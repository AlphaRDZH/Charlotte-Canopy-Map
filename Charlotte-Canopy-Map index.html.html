Data: Charlotte Urban Tree Canopy Assessment (PlanIT Geo for TreesCharlotte & City of Charlotte, Nov 2023), measured 2018 and 2022. Boundaries: City of Charlotte City Council Districts and Neighborhood Profile Areas (City of Charlotte GIS). Neighborhood colors: tree canopy from the Charlotte-Mecklenburg Quality of Life Explorer (2023); earlier years follow each district's measured trend. Projections after 2023: at the current pace every neighborhood loses canopy in proportion to what it has (city → 40% by 2050); under the 50% goal new canopy goes first to the hottest neighborhoods with the most room for trees, as the report recommends. Projected heat follows each neighborhood's canopy change, using the 2023 canopy–heat relationship across all neighborhoods. These are illustrations of the trend, not climate forecasts. 2023–2050 values are projections: the report's citywide planting scenarios (~6,500 trees/yr → 40% by 2050; ~40,462 trees/yr → 50%) applied evenly to every district. Heat: Trust for Public Land, Heat Severity – USA, yearly editions 2019–2023 (Landsat 8 surface temperature, 30 m). Each edition ranks heat relative to that year's city average, so it shows where heat concentrates, not absolute warming. 2018 uses the 2019 edition (summers 2018–19). District and neighborhood heat shares were calculated from these layers for this map.
